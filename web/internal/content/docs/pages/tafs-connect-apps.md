# Connect apps

**Connect apps** gives a taf one of your business's apps to work in.
For someone who manages Cards on the account; only they can connect an app.
Open it in Account or from a taf's Setup. The taf's Card still decides every action.

"Connects with its API key" means KIFF reads the app's published description and takes its key:

Not listed? Ask Coach. It can connect some other apps, and tells you when one isn't available yet.

## Connect one

1. **Pick the app**, then the taf it's for. A connection belongs to one taf.
2. **Approve on your phone** with Face ID or your fingerprint. The approval says, in words, what
   you're connecting.
3. **Sign in on the app's own page.** Your password goes to that app, never to tafs.

An app that uses a key takes it either on the approval sheet, sealed and never kept on the phone,
or on the connection service's page. The approval says which.

## What the taf can do with it

Coach picks the app's actions the job needs, and you approve them. Every call goes through KIFF and
is checked against the taf's Card, whatever the app's own consent screen allowed. See
[Held calls and approvals](/docs/approvals).

## App events

A taf can wake on something in a connected app, such as a new deal over €5,000. Coach sets it up in
[Setup › Starts when](/docs/tafs#the-app-s-screens). Nothing wakes the taf until you approve those
exact filters with Face ID.

For Slack as a way to give a taf work, see [Doors](/docs/tafs-doors#slack).

## Disconnect

On the taf's **Card** tab, **Disconnect** with Face ID takes the app's actions away at once; an
action already sent may still finish. If the app signs you out, the taf waits for **Sign in again**.

How KIFF checks an app's calls: [Connection profiles](/docs/tafs-connection-profiles).
