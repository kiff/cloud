# Who can do what

Everyone on a KIFF account is a **Viewer**, **Approver**, **Editor** or **Admin**.
For admins inviting people and sharing tafs.
Being on the account gives nothing on a taf: a person sees one once it's shared with them or they made it.

| Role | Sees tafs | Coach | New taf | Share a taf | Answers holds | Door conversations | Others' chats |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Viewer | shared with them | no | no | no | no | no | never |
| Approver | shared with them | no, for now | no | no | yes | no | never |
| Editor | shared, and the ones they made | on the tafs they made | yes | the tafs they made | yes* | on the tafs they made | never |
| Admin | all | yes | yes | yes | yes* | all | never |

\* Unless **separation of duties** is on: then no one answers a hold on their own request.

Every role can chat with the tafs it sees, and each person's chats are their own.

**Groups.** An admin can share a taf with a group. A group's manager adds or removes members, and
gets no other right on its tafs.

**What each person sees.** The Record, Activity and Needs you show a person their own use of a taf;
an Approver also sees the holds of tafs they use. Whoever manages a taf sees all its calls. Admins
see the whole account.

Name the account in Account before you invite anyone: invitees see its name, never your email.
KIFF checks every role on the service, not just in the app.
